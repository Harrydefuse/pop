# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Anyone who wants to get fitter and would keep going if the reward loop were better. The core is
people who game — some who need a reason to get off the sofa, some who already train hard and want
their effort to feed something — but the product is deliberately not gamer-only. It is a fitness app
whose rewards happen to be game rewards, and it should read that way to someone who has never
queued a ranked match.

The job to be done: turn showing up into something with visible, accumulating consequence. Most
people quit fitness apps because logging a workout produces a number and nothing else.

## Product Purpose

LVL100 is a game, not a tracker with points bolted on. Activity is the controller: verified physical
effort is the only input, and everything it drives — levels, rank, stats, loot, pets, skins — is
game progression. The spine is a story mode. Nine bosses across three acts, each gated behind a
level, each taking damage from the sessions you log, each dropping something you cannot get any
other way. World raids exist and everyone fights them together, but they are side content. The main
line is your own run through the game, and it has an ending.

Health-provider verification is what makes any of it mean anything; anyone can type a number, so
typed numbers pay half and never touch a leaderboard.

Success is the streak: people opening it on day 40 because they are four thousand HP into THE WALL,
not because a notification guilted them.

## Positioning

A game you play by moving. You do not have to quit gaming to get your life on track — this is the
one where getting fitter is how you finish the game.

## Marketing surface

The website is a brand surface even though the project's default register is product. Its job is to
get a stranger into the live prototype.

- Primary CTA: try the live prototype. Secondary: keep reading / see how it works.
- The line a visitor remembers after ten seconds: **don't quit gaming to get fit**.
- Belief ladder: this is a real, working thing → the rewards are genuinely desirable → I can't cheat
  it, so progress means something → it works for me even if I'm not a hardcore gamer → I can try it
  right now, for free, with no account.
- Proof on hand: the working prototype itself, and screenshots of it in `public/shots/`. No
  testimonials, press or logos yet — do not invent any.

## Brand Personality

Warm and encouraging. A friend who noticed you've been sitting a lot, not a coach shouting about
excuses. It celebrates small wins, never shames a missed day, and is honest that games are a
legitimate thing to love rather than a habit to quit.

Three words: warm, sturdy, kinetic.

## Anti-references

All four were named explicitly, and the site must avoid every one:

- Generic SaaS landing pages — gradient hero, three identical feature cards, logo bar, pricing table.
- Aggressive fitness marketing — shredded abs, before/afters, "no excuses", shame as motivation.
- Crypto / NFT gaming sites — neon-on-black with floating 3D and play-to-earn energy. A live risk
  given the pixel art and loot vocabulary, so the site must not simply enlarge the app's dark UI.
- Corporate health-tech — stock joggers, soft blues, insurance-brochure calm.

## Design Principles

**Show the working thing.** There is a real prototype; every claim on the site should be backed by
the actual product rather than an illustration of it.

**Permission, not pressure.** The product's whole thesis is that you do not have to give something
up. Copy and imagery never trade in guilt.

**Earned, not given.** Delayed gratification is the core mechanic — sealed chests, month-long stone
milestones, a streak. The brand should feel patient, not urgent.

**Legible to outsiders.** Anyone who has never played a competitive game should understand every
screen. Game vocabulary is explained by what it does, never assumed.

**Integrity is the feature.** Verification is what separates this from a typing contest, so it is
stated plainly rather than buried.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text at 4.5:1 and large text at 3:1 minimum, keyboard reachable throughout,
visible focus states, `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured with a real alternative rather than a
removal, and no meaning carried by colour alone.
